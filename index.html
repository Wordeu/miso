01 / HOW IT WORKS

An outage elsewhere should not stop your essential work.

Many organisations rely on remote systems for their daily operations. A cloud outage, cyber attack or damage to infrastructure can interrupt that work. We’re developing Orla to help essential services continue on local computers when those connections are lost.

Each computer in the network is a node. Our software is being designed to let these nodes communicate securely, share available computing power and put the most important work first. The aim is a network that is simple to set up and can adapt when resources become limited.

01

Keep work running locally.

If the cloud becomes unavailable, the network is designed to keep serving essential workloads that have been set up to run locally. Those tasks need their data and software available on site.

02

Share work between nodes.

If one node fails, others would take over the work they are able to run. This depends on compatible hardware and software, access to the right data and enough spare computing power.

03

Protect the most important tasks.

When computing power becomes limited, the software would pause lower-priority processing to leave more room for essential functions. Your team would decide which tasks matter most.

02 / WHY NOW

Local intelligence is becoming more practical.

Our starting point is that smaller, more efficient AI models will make it possible to do more useful work on local hardware. At the same time, organisations want more control over where their data lives and how their services cope with disruption.

We’re starting with the work that cannot wait.

Our first focus is helping organisations continue essential work through disruption. We’re speaking with teams to understand which services need this most, what their current systems cannot cover and what they would pay for a better approach.

Over time, we want local networks to work together across organisations and infrastructure. For now, we’re focused on finding the right first use case and testing what it takes to keep it running.
